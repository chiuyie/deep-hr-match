# Deep HR Match — scoring brief

**Audience:** leadership  
**Date:** 28 October 2026  
**Status:** current product behaviour

---

## How a match result is decided

Matching runs in two steps.

1. **Filters decide who appears.** If the employer sets a requirement and the candidate does not meet it, or leaves that answer blank, that person is removed from the list. They are not scored.
2. **The score ranks the people who remain.** The only score is the **7^7 matching language** — how closely the candidate’s seven factor answers match the job’s seven factor answers.

Salary, CV skills, years of experience, education, desired job titles, and preferred locations do **not** change the score. They can still appear after an unlock.

The list keeps the **top 25**. Unlocking a profile (S$10) is separate from the score.

---

## What the score measures

Both the employer and the candidate answer the same seven factors. For each factor, matching compares the chosen words. Agreement raises the score; disagreement lowers it.

The overall match score is a **percentage from 0 to 100**. It is the weighted average of the seven factor scores.

---

## Factor weights (current)

| Factor | Weight |
|--------|--------|
| Character – Roles | 14% |
| Experience | 14% |
| Knowledge – Domain | 14% |
| Motivations | 14% |
| **Skills – Function** | **16%** |
| Talent | 14% |
| Values | 14% |

**Skills – Function** carries a slightly higher weight than each of the other six factors, so agreement or disagreement on that factor moves the overall score a little more.

The seven weights add up to 100%. If a factor cannot be compared on both sides, it is left out and the remaining factors keep their relative weights.

---

## How to read the result

| Score band | Meaning for the employer |
|------------|--------------------------|
| 80–100 | Strong alignment on the matching language |
| 50–79 | Mixed alignment — review the factor differences |
| Below 50 | Weak alignment — use gaps to guide interview questions |

A high score means the **matching language** lines up. It does not mean the profile is more complete, and it does not add points for meeting filters. Meeting a filter only keeps the candidate in the list.

---

## Example

If six factors match fully and only **Skills – Function** differs, the overall score is about **84%**.  
If six factors match fully and a different factor (for example Character – Roles) differs, the overall score is about **86%**.

That is the intended effect of the Skills – Function boost.

---

## What employers and candidates should know

- Fill filter fields the employer cares about. A blank on an active filter removes the candidate.
- Complete all seven factors. Incomplete factors reduce what can be compared.
- Regenerating matches refreshes the ranked list. Existing unlocks are not revoked.
